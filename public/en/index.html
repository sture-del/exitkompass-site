                  Free business valuation for cross-border M&A in Europe – how Exitkompass works

                  Exitkompass gives you a free, indicative valuation of an unlisted limited company in Sweden, Norway, Finland, the Netherlands, Poland or Lithuania, much like the first assessment a buyer or adviser would make. It is built for owners who are thinking about selling, now or in a few years, and for buyers and advisers looking at companies across borders. Choose the country at the top, enter the registration number or company name, and we fetch the latest filed accounts from the national business register and fill in the figures for you. Where only part of the figures is public, the valuation is made on the data available.

                  The valuation uses the three multiples most used in acquisitions: EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT and EV/revenue. The EBITDA multiple reflects what the business generates, the EBIT multiple takes the company's own depreciation into account and so how capital-intensive it is, and the revenue multiple weighs more when the margin is low. The multiples start from industry levels and the market, and are adjusted for interest rates (the Riksbank, Norges Bank, the National Bank of Poland or the ECB for euro countries), the business cycle in the industry and how the company's margin compares with its industry. Multiples are set per market: Finland follows the Nordic level, the Netherlands is about 10% higher, and Poland and Lithuania about 15% lower, reflecting what buyers pay in each market. The multiple is then adjusted for what buyers pay for: customer spread, recurring revenue, owner dependency, growth, written contracts and the quality of financial reporting. Net cash is added, so the result is a range for the equity value.

                  You also get an exit readiness score and the actions that raise the value most. The full report, available in English, adds a sensitivity analysis, a 12–24 month value creation plan and the types of buyer that pay the most for a company like yours.

                  Frequently asked questions about valuing and selling a company

                  What is my company worth?

                  In practice a company is worth what a buyer is willing to pay. For small and medium-sized unlisted companies the starting point is usually earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation (EBITDA) multiplied by a multiple that depends on industry, size and risk. In Exitkompass the base multiples range from about 4 to 8.5 times EBITDA depending on the industry, according to the Dealsuite Nordic M&A Monitor, which compiles deals reported by Nordic M&A advisers. Smaller companies are valued lower than larger ones, and the multiple is then adjusted for the company's own strengths and weaknesses.

                  Is the valuation based on historical figures or on a budget?

                  On historical figures from the annual reports, unless you upload something else. If you upload monthly reports, a budget or a business plan, they are weighted into the report. The monthly reports give the last twelve months, which get the largest weight in the earnings base. The budget gives a growth value weighted in at 20%, plus 5% if a business plan explains it and plus 10% if results so far this year are on budget. If results are more than 15% behind, 10% is deducted, and if the budget grows much faster than history, the weight is halved. The weight is at most 35%, because buyers mainly pay for proven earnings.

                  Is Exitkompass suitable for large companies and groups?

                  Exitkompass is built for small and medium-sized companies with revenue up to about 50 million euros (500 million SEK or NOK), where multiples for unlisted companies give a reasonable first assessment. Smaller groups are valued on the consolidated figures. For larger companies and groups, value is driven by listed peers and strategic transactions, so the tool shows no automatic valuation there and instead offers a free meeting with a partner at Adsco Capital Partners.

                  Which countries does Exitkompass cover?

                  Sweden, Norway, Finland, the Netherlands, Poland and Lithuania. We fetch accounts from each national register: Bolagsverket and Allabolag in Sweden, the Brønnøysund Register Centre in Norway, the Finnish Patent and Registration Office (PRH), the Dutch Chamber of Commerce (KVK), the Polish National Court Register (KRS) and the Lithuanian Registry Centre. Small Dutch companies often file only a balance sheet, so you add revenue and EBITDA yourself. Amounts are shown in the local currency: SEK, NOK, PLN or euros. The tool is available in English, Swedish, Norwegian, Finnish, Dutch, Polish and Lithuanian, and you can choose any language for any country.

                  Which multiples does Exitkompass use – EBITDA, EBIT or revenue?

                  All three, because that is how buyers calculate in acquisitions. EV/EBITDA is the most common for small and medium-sized companies. EV/EBIT gives a fairer picture for capital-intensive companies with large depreciation, and EV/revenue is used when profitability is low or temporarily weak. The earnings part is weighted 60% EBITDA and 40% EBIT, and revenue gets 20–50% depending on the margin.

                  What is the difference between enterprise value and equity value?

                  Enterprise value is the value of the business itself, regardless of how it is financed. Equity value is what the owners receive: enterprise value plus cash minus interest-bearing debt. Exitkompass shows both.

                  What raises the value before a sale?

                  Buyers pay more for companies with spread and recurring revenue, that run without the owner, have written contracts with customers and key people, grow steadily and have clear, reliable financial reporting. Exitkompass shows which of these actions matter most for your company.

                  Can I use the valuation for divorce, probate or a dispute?

                  No. Exitkompass is built for a sale and shows what a buyer might pay for the company, that is the going-concern market value based on multiples. Divorce, estate settlement, inheritance, disputes, minority buy-outs and tax matters often require other methods, such as net asset value, and a formal valuation.

                  How does Exitkompass calculate the valuation?

                  We use three multiples – EV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT and EV/revenue – on a weighted average of the last few years' earnings, adjusted for customer concentration, share of recurring revenue, owner dependence, growth, contracts and reporting. When margins are low, revenue carries more weight. The multiples also follow the interest rate level, the business cycle in the industry and the company's margin compared with its industry, and they are set per market. Finally, net cash is added to arrive at the equity value.

                  How is a limited company valued when it is sold?

                  In a sale, the company is valued on what a buyer is willing to pay: normalised earnings over the last few years times an industry multiple, adjusted for risk and quality. Cash is then added and interest-bearing debt deducted to get the value of the shares. Exitkompass calculates this for free and shows what raises the value before a sale.

                  How accurate is the valuation?

                  The valuation is indicative and based on historical figures and your answers. What a buyer actually pays depends on, among other things, market conditions, the type of buyer, financing, due diligence and the terms of the deal. Use it as a starting point and talk to an adviser before making decisions.
